# Scotland (researched 2026-11-08)

Scope: a patient harmed by care at a **public** hospital (here: the Emergency Department at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh (RIE), run by **NHS Lothian**, missed a scaphoid fracture; the patient now needs surgery) who wants (a) answers and an apology and (b) possibly compensation.
Every fact has an official source link. URL status checked with `curl -sL` (browser user agent) on 2026-11-07/08: **verified** (fetched, HTTP 200) / **not verified** / **broken**. No form was submitted and no visible browser was opened.

**Route in one line:** complain to the **NHS Lothian Patient Experience Team** (online form, email, phone or post) within **6 months** of finding out (and no later than **12 months** after the event) -> Stage 1 early resolution (**5 working days**) or Stage 2 investigation (acknowledge in **3**, respond in **20 working days**) -> if still unhappy, **Scottish Public Services Ombudsman (SPSO)** within **12 months** -> compensation only through a **civil damages action** (sheriff court up to £100,000, otherwise Court of Session; **3-year limitation**; NHS defended by the **Central Legal Office**, indemnified by **CNORIS**), funded by **civil legal aid** (available in Scotland for medical negligence) or a capped **success fee agreement**, with **QOCS** protection.

**Surprises the agent must handle:**
1. **SPSO is degraded right now.** A "significant outage" of its casework management system: news item posted 4 November 2026 headed "Friday 6 November: Service update" says "Following a significant outage, we now have access to our casework management system" and asks people "please do not contact us for updates on your case at this time." The form itself is still reachable (Next button present).
2. **There is still no compulsory clinical negligence pre-action protocol in Scotland.** The 2016 compulsory PI protocol expressly excludes injuries that "arise from alleged clinical negligence". The SCJC's draft Annual Report 2025/26 (June 2026 meeting papers) still lists "Pre Action Protocol – for Clinical Negligence" only in a backlog of suggestions to be prioritised. An agent that quotes the England & Wales Pre-Action Protocol for the Resolution of Clinical Disputes, a "Letter of Claim" with 4 months to respond, or the Limitation Act 1980 is wrong for Scotland.
3. **No NHS redress scheme.** Unlike Wales (£50,000 redress) there is no in-process compensation: the NHS Model CHP says "The NHS complaints procedure does not provide for financial compensation", and the SPSO "cannot award you compensation for losses or damages in the way that a Court or Tribunal can".
4. **Legal aid IS available** for medical negligence in Scotland (mygov.scot lists "trying to get compensation for injuries after an accident or for medical negligence" as a civil case), unlike adult clinical negligence in England and Wales.
5. **NHS Lothian's own pages disagree on the time limit.** Its FAQ says "we ask that you contact us within 12 months of you having reason to complain", but its "How long do I have" page and the statute say six months from awareness, capped at 12 months from the event. Use the statutory rule.
6. **NHS Lothian's Patient Experience Team postal address differs** between the web page (Waverley Court, 4 East Market Street, Edinburgh EH8 8BG) and the January 2025 public-facing CHP PDF (Mainpoint, 102 Westport, Edinburgh EH3 9DN). Use the web page (current); email/online form avoids the problem.
7. **NICE guidelines are not formally Scottish guidance** (SIGN is), but there is no SIGN scaphoid guideline, so NICE NG38 is the national-level reference an expert would use.

## 2. Complaint to the provider (NHS Lothian, NHS Model Complaints Handling Procedure)

**Law:** Patient Rights (Scotland) Act 2011 and the Patient Rights (Complaints Procedure and Consequential Provisions) (Scotland) Regulations 2012 (SSI 2012/36), as amended from 1 April 2017. All NHS boards use the SPSO-published **NHS Model Complaints Handling Procedure (MCHP)**.

**Time limit (reg 5 SSI 2012/36):** "a complaint is raised orally or in writing within six months from the date on which the subject matter of the complaint comes to the complainant's notice, provided that is no later than twelve months after the date on which the subject matter of the complaint occurred." Reg 5(2): "The periods specified in paragraph (1) may be extended if the feedback and complaints officer ... considers it would be reasonable in the circumstances."
NHS Lothian: "Normally, you must make your complaint within six months of: The event you want to complain about, or Finding out you have a reason to complain, but no longer than 12 months after the event itself." "A complaint can sometimes be accepted after the time limit." "You can complain to the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman about an NHS decision not to accept your complaint."

**Stages (NHS MCHP; NHS Lothian "What happens when I complain?"):**
1. **Stage 1 Early Resolution:** NHS Lothian: "within five working days, unless there are exceptional circumstances." MCHP: extension of "no more than five additional working days"; "All attempts to resolve the complaint at this stage must take no longer than ten working days from the date you receive the complaint." Serious/complex complaints can go straight to Stage 2.
2. **Stage 2 Investigation:** MCHP: "complaints must be acknowledged within three working days" and "you should provide a full response to the complaint as soon as possible but not later than 20 working days, unless an extension is required." "There is no flexibility within the Patient Rights (Scotland) Act 2011 to 'stop the clock'". NHS Lothian FAQ: "We acknowledge all feedback within 3 working days of receiving it" and "we aim to provide a written response within 20 days."
3. The Stage 2 response must tell the person about the SPSO ("If you remain dissatisfied ... you can ask the SPSO to look at your complaint").
- MCHP on legal action: "You should offer to resolve someone's complaint using the NHS complaints procedure, even where the person has stated ... that they intend to take legal proceedings." But if the person "clearly intends to take legal action", the board may decide not to apply the procedure.
- MCHP on money: "The NHS complaints procedure does not provide for financial compensation. The independent Patient Advice and Support Service may be able to advise anyone who is seeking compensation where to get information about specialist solicitors". It suggests AvMA or the Law Society of Scotland.

**How to complain to NHS Lothian (Patient Experience Team, PET):** "Online By completing our online feedback form By email LOTH.Feedback@nhs.scot In writing Patient Experience Team – NHS Lothian Waverley Court, 4 East Market Street, Edinburgh, EH8 8BG By telephone 0131 536 3370 (open Mon-Fri, 9am to 2pm)". ## 4. Compensation route

**Only route: a civil action of damages for clinical (medical) negligence.** No NHS redress or no-fault scheme in Scotland (MCHP and SPSO quotes above).

- **Courts:** Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 s39 ("Exclusive competence"): where "the aggregate total value of all such orders sought, exclusive of interest and expenses, does not exceed £100,000", proceedings "may be brought only in the sheriff court". Above that, the **Court of Session** (Edinburgh). The **National Personal Injury Court** (SCTS's current name for the All-Scotland Sheriff Personal Injury Court, "established on 22 September 2015") is a Scotland-wide specialist sheriff court for PI claims; its value threshold was not stated on the page (commonly £5,000+; **not verified**).
- **Limitation:** Prescription and Limitation (Scotland) Act 1973 s17(2): "no action to which this section applies shall be brought unless it is commenced within a period of 3 years after— (a) the date on which the injuries were sustained ... or (b) the date (if later ...) on which the pursuer in the action became, or on which, in the opinion of the court, it would have been reasonably practicable for him ... to become, aware of all the following facts— (i) that the injuries in question were sufficiently serious to justify his bringing an action of damages ...; (ii) that the injuries were attributable in whole or in part to an act or omission; and (iii) that the defender was a person to whose act or omission the injuries were attributable". s19A: the court "may, if it seems to it equitable to do so, allow him to bring the action notwithstanding that provision." Complaints and SPSO do **not** stop the clock.
- **Pre-action rules:** **none compulsory for clinical negligence.** Act of Sederunt SSI 2016/215 (compulsory PI Pre-Action Protocol from 28 November 2016) does not apply where the injuries "(i) arise from alleged clinical negligence". SCJC draft Annual Report 2025/26 lists "Pre Action Protocol – for Clinical Negligence" among backlog suggestions (not made). In practice: solicitor requests records, obtains expert reports (breach of duty, causation), intimates the claim to the board / CLO.
- **Who defends:** NHS boards are members of **CNORIS** (Clinical Negligence and Other Risks Indemnity Scheme), managed by NHS National Services Scotland; CNORIS annual report 2024-25: "NHS National Services Scotland (NSS) manages the Scheme on behalf of the Scottish Government, and the NHS Central Legal Office provides legal advice and guidance to members in relation to negligence claims." Reimbursement applies where total costs exceed the "CNORIS £25,000 deductibles threshold". So a claim against NHS Lothian is handled by the **Central Legal Office (CLO)**.
- **Duty of candour** (section 9) is separate from liability: an apology under it is not an admission (Apologies (Scotland) Act 2016; not researched further).

## 5. Funding

- **Civil legal aid (SLAB):** available for medical negligence. Tests (mygov.scot): financial; "is there a legal basis to take your case forward?" (probable cause); "it's reasonable to spend public money to support your case"; "help is not available to you from somewhere else". Apply only via a legal aid solicitor. Limits from 6 April 2026 (SLAB civil keycard): disposable income lower £3,521 / upper £26,239 a year; disposable capital lower £7,853 / upper £13,017; contributions 33% / 50% / 100% by income band. Clawback: "you may have to pay costs back later if you keep or gain money or property."
- **Advice and Assistance** (initial advice, letters, records, negotiation; not court): from 6 April 2026 "£245 a week maximum for eligibility" income and "£1,716 maximum for eligibility" capital.
- **Online estimators (no login):** civil legal aid estimator (Gravity Forms, 5 steps: benefits Yes/No, partner, dependants 0-10, income/outgoings, "Total Disposable Capital"; "this estimator is only a guide") and Advice and Assistance estimator.
- **Success fee agreements ("no win no fee"):** Civil Litigation (Expenses and Group Proceedings) (Scotland) Act 2018 and SSI 2020/110 reg 2(3), PI cap (incl. VAT): "in respect of the first £100,000 of the financial benefit, 20%", "over £100,000 but not exceeding £500,000, 10%", "over £500,000, 2.5%". s6(2): in a PI SFA the client "is not liable to make any payment (including outlays ...) ... apart from the success fee, regardless of whether any damages are obtained" (genuine no win no fee, outlays included). Future-loss damages are excluded from the fee base unless conditions in s6(4)-(5) are met.
- **QOCS:** 2018 Act s8 (in force 30 June 2021): in PI claims conducted "in an appropriate manner", "The court must not make an award of expenses against the person" (lost for fraud, "manifestly unreasonable" behaviour or abuse of process).

## 7. Clinical guidelines body

- **SIGN** (Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network, part of **Healthcare Improvement Scotland**) writes Scotland's national clinical guidelines. SIGN's list has **no scaphoid / wrist-fracture guideline** (only hip fracture and osteoporosis/fragility fractures).
