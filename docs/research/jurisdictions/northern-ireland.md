# Northern Ireland (researched 2026-11-07)

Scope: a patient harmed by care at a **public** hospital (e.g. a missed scaphoid fracture in the Royal Victoria Hospital emergency department, Belfast) who wants (a) answers and an apology and (b) possibly compensation.
Every fact has an official source link. URL status checked with `curl -sL` (browser user agent) on 2026-11-07: **verified** (fetched, HTTP 200 and content read) / **not verified** / **broken**. No form was submitted; no visible browser was opened.

**Route in one line:** complaint to the HSC Trust that runs the hospital (online form, no login; Stage 1 then Stage 2) -> Northern Ireland Public Services Ombudsman (NIPSO), within 6 months of the Trust's final letter (online form **currently down**: email, phone, post or PDF form) -> compensation only through a clinical negligence claim (letter of claim under the High Court Protocol, then the High Court or County Court; 3-year limitation). Funding: civil legal aid (applied for by a solicitor) or a solicitor working "on spec". **Conditional fee agreements are not available.** Free help throughout: Patient and Client Council.

Key points for the agent:
- **The complaints rules changed on 1 January 2026.** All HSC bodies now use NIPSO's **Model Complaints Handling Procedure (MCHP)**, with two stages: Stage 1 within 5 working days, Stage 2 within 20 working days. It replaced the Department of Health (DoH) 2023 guidance. Some nidirect text still describes the old rules ("normally no longer than 12 months after the event").
- **The NIPSO online complaints form is unavailable.** A NIPSO homepage notice dated 4 November 2026 says: "Our online complaints form is currently unavailable. However you can still make a complaint via email, on the phone, or in person."
- **No win, no fee is unlawful in NI for court work.** Regulation 17 of the Law Society's Solicitors' Practice Regulations bans contingency fees. Article 38 of the 2003 Order (CFAs) has never been brought into force.
- **Clinical negligence is still covered by legal aid in NI**, unlike England. You cannot apply yourself; a solicitor applies.

## 2. Complaint to the provider

**Rules (from 1 Jan 2026): NIPSO Health and Social Care MCHP.**
- "Published on 1 July 2025, it replaces the Department of Health's HSC Complaints Procedure Directions and Guidance on 1 January 2026." (NIPSO MCHP PDF, p.5) https://www.nipso.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/NIPSO_MCHP_Health%26SocialCare.pdf (verified)
- DoH page: "From 1 January 2026, the NIPSO Model Complaints Handling Procedure applies to all HSC organisations. Complaints raised before that date stay under the Department of Health guidance until they are concluded." https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/publications/hsc-complaints-standards-and-guidelines (verified; paraphrased by fetch tool)
- **Time limit to complain:** "NIPSO requires organisations to permit complaints to be raised up to 6 months after the events occurred or the service user becoming aware of the issue. However, organisations must not apply the time limit rigidly" (MCHP s.1.8). The Trusts use the same wording, for example South Eastern: "Normally you must make your complaint within six months of: the event you want to complain about, or finding out that you have a reason to complain." https://setrust.hscni.net/contact-us/complaints/who-can-complain/ (verified)
- **Stage 1 (frontline):** "Complaint resolved or response provided within 5 working days (can be extended to another 5 working days, if required)."
- **Stage 2 (investigation):** "Complaint acknowledged within 3 working days. Complaint resolved or response provided within 20 working days (can be extended if required)." Moving from Stage 1 to Stage 2: "NIPSO requires a minimum period of 30 days". Belfast Trust: "within 30 days of receiving the outcome at Stage 1."
- **Outdated text to beware of:** nidirect still says "usually within six months of you becoming aware ... normally no longer than 12 months after the event" and "full response within 20 working days". https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/how-complain-or-raise-concerns-about-health-services (verified). Western Trust's form still has a field "If there has been a delay of more than 12 months in telling us of your complaint, please s[tate why]".
- A complaint to the Trust gives answers and an apology, not money. The High Court Protocol, para 13: these procedures "are not designed to provide compensation for cases of negligence."

## 3. Escalation: Northern Ireland Public Services Ombudsman (NIPSO)

- Free. NIPSO covers HSC Trusts and can consider clinical judgement: "We can also consider complaints about professional judgement and clinical decisions which have been made by health and social care professionals." https://www.nipso.org.uk/make-complaint/how-we-deal-your-complaint (verified)
- **Prerequisite:** "Before you make a complaint to us you should normally have: Complained directly to the organisation; Gone through its complaints process; Received a final response to your complaint." https://www.nipso.org.uk/make-complaint (verified)
- **Time limit:** "within 6 months since you received correspondence from [the organisation] informing you that the complaints handling procedure is complete and of your right to refer your complaint to NIPSO." (MCHP template letter, repeated by Trusts) NIPSO "would generally not investigate your complaint if: it has been made more than six months since the complaint was dealt with by the public body ... **you intend to take the issue to court**." (how-we-deal page)

## 4. Compensation route

- **No redress or no-fault scheme for HSC.** Compensation means a clinical negligence claim against the Trust in the civil courts.
- **Courts:** the High Court (King's Bench Division, Royal Courts of Justice, Belfast) or a County Court. The County Court limit is **£30,000** (County Courts (Financial Limits) Order (NI) 2013, in operation 25 Feb 2013): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2013/18/article/2/made (verified). **A pending change:** the DoJ plans to raise the limit to £60,000. A draft 2026 Order was before the Justice Committee (Clyde & Co, Oct 2026: "targeting implementation during autumn 2026 ... late November is currently anticipated"). https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/northern-ireland-county-court-reform-reshaping (verified; law-firm source; status **not verified** officially). AvMA: clinical negligence cases "will usually proceed in the High Court because of their complexity or value."
- **Pre-action rules: the "Protocol for Clinical Negligence Litigation" (High Court)**, in effect from 1 October 2021 (signed 19 June 2023 by the Lady Chief Justice). It replaces the 2012 Protocol and the 2009 Pre-Action Protocol. https://www.judiciaryni.uk/judicial-decisions/protocol-clinical-negligence-litigation-high-court (verified; the PDF is a scanned image: https://www.judiciaryni.uk/files/judiciaryni/decisions/Protocol%20for%20Clinical%20Negligence%20Litigation%20in%20the%20High%20Court%20%282%29.pdf, verified, read by rendering the pages)
  - Litigants in person: "If a party to a claim is acting without legal representation, they should still, in so far as is reasonably possible, comply with the terms of this Protocol."
  - Two steps: "Firstly, there will be a record request ... Records may be requested directly from the record-holder(s) without the need for a Letter of Claim. ... Secondly, when records have been obtained and independent expert evidence has been secured, the intended plaintiff should serve a Letter of Claim" (para 6).
  - Records: "supplied by the relevant healthcare provider free of charge, within 1 month"; can be extended "up to 3 months in total where the notes are complex or numerous" (para 8).
  - Letter of claim: acknowledge "within 14 days of receipt"; "No later than 4 months ... from the date of receipt of the letter of claim" the Trust should send a reasoned response admitting or denying liability (para 25). "proceedings should not generally be issued until after 4 months" (para 24).
  - Sanctions: "The Court may consider costs penalties and/or procedural penalties for non-compliance" (para 26). Aim: resolve "within 48 months of the date of issue of the writ". Standstill agreements are encouraged where limitation is close (para 21).
  - The Law Society has template letters (letter of claim, response, record request), per the Protocol at "https://www.lawsoc-ni.org/clinical-negligence-protocol". That link is **broken (404)** today.
- County Court version: "Protocol for Clinical Negligence Litigation in the County Court" (1 June 2023) and Practice Direction 01/2023. https://www.judiciaryni.uk/judicial-decisions/protocol-clinical-negligence-litigation-county-court (verified; the PDF is scanned, contents **not read**)
- **Limitation:** Limitation (NI) Order 1989, Art. 7(4): "three years from— (a) the date on which the cause of action accrued, or (b) the date of knowledge (if later) of the person injured." The court can disapply this under Art. 50. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/1989/1339/article/7 (verified). AvMA adds that for children the period does not start "until their 18th birthday."

## 5. Funding

**Legal aid (Legal Services Agency NI, an agency of the DoJ):**
- **Clinical negligence is in scope.** Schedule 2 of the Access to Justice (NI) Order 2003 ("Civil legal services: excluded services", in operation 1.4.2015) does not exclude personal injury or clinical negligence, and representation in the High Court and County Court is fundable. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/2003/435/schedule/2 (verified). DoJ has a dedicated FAQ: "How do I make an application for a clinical negligence claim? You will need to make an application to representation higher." It also says "Generally, the Agency will limit the certificate to certain steps in proceedings", and an expert report needs "the express prior written consent of the Legal Services Agency". https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/articles/legal-aid-faqs-clinical-negligence-claims (verified)
- Must pass a merits test and a means test (Civil Legal Services (Financial) Regulations (NI) 2015: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2015/196/contents, verified). Some benefits (e.g. Universal Credit) passport you through the means test.
- **How to apply:** "All applications for legal aid should be made by a solicitor." nidirect: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/legal-aid-schemes (verified). **We found no public online eligibility checker.** LSANI contacts: https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/contacts/lsani-contact-details (verified)

**No win, no fee (checked carefully):**
- **Contingency fees are banned for court work.** Solicitors' Practice Regulations 1987 (as amended to Sept 2022), reg. 17: "A solicitor shall not accept instructions in respect of any claim ... under any arrangement whereby he will receive, in respect of such claim or matter a contingency fee". The definition: "'contingency fee' means a fee for services rendered in connection with contentious business which is only payable in the event of the proceedings to which the services relate being successful". https://lawsoc-ni.org/assets/general/files/regulations-and-standards/1987-Practice-Regs-as-amended-to-Sept-2022.pdf (verified, PDF text read)
- **The statute was never commenced.** Article 38 of the Access to Justice (NI) Order 2003 (CFAs) exists on legislation.gov.uk: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/2003/435/article/38 (verified). However, DoJ's Access to Justice Review Part 2 (Sept 2015) says "the 2003 Order, Article 38 (not yet in force)" and "It is hard to see why Northern Ireland should be the only part of the United Kingdom which denies its citizens the option of properly regulated conditional fees". It recommended "CFAs are made available in Northern Ireland by bringing into operation Article 38". https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/publications/doj/access-to-justice-review-part-2-report.pdf (verified). We found **no evidence that it has been commenced since**. Note: legislation.gov.uk does not mark Article 38 as prospective, so this is **not verified** from a 2026 primary source.
- **What happens in practice:** the same review says "most claims are pursued 'on spec' on the express or implied understanding that the solicitor will not pursue the client for costs if the claim is unsuccessful, although the client will usually be expected to fund the disbursements". It also says "Success fees are not recoverable in litigation under the current law in Northern Ireland." AvMA (NI guide): "No win no fee agreements in Northern Ireland are unlawful." https://www.avma.org.uk/resource/legal-action-bringing-a-claim-in-clinical-medical-negligence-in-northern-ireland/ (verified)
- **Agent wording:** do not tell an NI user "no win no fee"; say "legal aid, or ask whether the solicitor will act on a speculative basis".

## 7. Clinical guidelines body

- NI has no guideline body of its own for this. **NICE clinical guidelines apply once DoH endorses them.** DoH: guidance is "locally reviewed for applicability to Northern Ireland and, where appropriate, endorsed for implementation in HSC". The current process is Circular HSC (SQSD) 9/26, "effective from 1 July 2026". https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/articles/nice-clinical-guidelines (verified)
- **NG38 "Fractures (non-complex): assessment and management" (NICE, published 17 February 2016) is endorsed for NI** by Circular HSC (SQSD) (NICE NG38) 22/16, dated 15 April 2016: "The Department has recently reviewed the above NICE guidance and has formally endorsed it as applicable in Northern Ireland." Trusts were to "Implement the Guideline within a further 9 months". Endorsement list: https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/articles/nice-endorsed-clinical-guidelines-20152016 (verified). Circular: https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/publications/dhssps/hsc-sqsd-nice-ng38-22-16_2.pdf (verified). The old link on the 2016/17 list (`http://www.dhsspsni.gov.uk/...`) redirects to the DoH homepage, so it is **broken**.
